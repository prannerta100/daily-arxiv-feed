{\small\textit{MT eval is saturating; LTB is a living adversarial benchmark with item-level checklists so failures stay hard, reproducible, and actionable.}}\\[1pt]
{\footnotesize Proposes Last Translation Benchmark: community-submitted, human-reviewed ``model-breaking'' translation items across text/image/audio/video, each with explicit pass/fail verification rules instead of a single static score. {\small\textbf{Typological Feature Prediction with Large Language Models: An In-Context Learning Approach}} {\scriptsize[\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03775}{arxiv}]}\\
{\scriptsize Qianwen Wang, York Hay Ng, Aditya Khan, En-Shiun Annie Lee}\\

{\small\textit{LLMs predict missing typological features well only when given explicit evidence from related/nearby languages, and their rationales usually follow that evidence.}}\\[1pt]
{\footnotesize Casts typology completion as evidence-conditioned in-context reasoning: language-name-only prompts are weak, but adding phylogenetic (family/genus) and areal (neighbor) cues makes LLMs strong; also evaluates rationale faithfulness. Use URIEL+/Glottolog to build prompts listing neighbor feature values for a target language; ask the LLM to infer a missing feature and justify it; compare zero-shot vs evidence-augmented ICL and check rationale--evidence consistency. Zero-shot underperforms; adding phylogenetic+geographic evidence yields large gains, beating non-LLM baselines without penalizing low-resource languages, and explanations are typically consistent with the provided neighbor evidence.}

{\small\textbf{Lost in Reordering: Structural Sensitivity of Multilingual LLMs under Semantics-Preserving Perturbations}} {\scriptsize[\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03511}{arxiv}]}\\
{\scriptsize Karthika Nhayakkat, Rajat Verma, Maharaj Brahma, Vetcha Gnana Mahesh, Maunendra Sankar Desarkar, Ganesh Ramakrishnan, Rohit Saluja}\\

{\small\textit{Multilingual LLMs are brittle to meaning-preserving reordering/voice changes in free word-order languages, causing sharp reasoning drops.}}\\[1pt]
{\footnotesize Introduces IndicReStruct: Hindi/Malayalam GSM8K-style problems rewritten with linguistically grounded, semantics-preserving structural transformations, exposing sensitivity to surface syntax over invariant meaning. Apply constituent reordering and active↔passive voice; evaluate six LLMs across prompting on original vs perturbed inputs; use residual-stream activation patching to localize where representations fail and where patching restores accuracy. Accuracy drops are large and consistent across models/prompts; errors often misbind numbers to entities. Mid-layer activation patching recovers more than early/late patching, implying entity--quantity alignment is formed (and disrupted) in intermediate layers.}

{\small\textbf{How Far Can Synthetic Data Take Thai OCR?}} {\scriptsize[\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03595}{arxiv}]}\\
{\scriptsize Kunat Pipatanakul}\\

{\small\textit{Synthetic-only Thai OCR can nearly match real-label training if you model the right realism factors (fonts, layout, handwriting glyphs).}}\\[1pt]
{\footnotesize Breaks synthetic ``realism'' into controllable ingredients, identifies which drive transfer, and uses the best recipe to adapt a 0.9B VLM OCR model to Thai with zero real Thai OCR labels (Wayu-Paxa-OCR-Zero). Document reconstruction pipeline toggling font diversity, 2D layout, non-text context, source-domain matching, and handwriting glyph variation; compare page-level vs crop-level supervision; adapt PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 using 45,723 synthetic pages. Non-text context helps little; font diversity, explicit layout, and real handwriting glyphs are key. Source-domain matching helps for page-level but can hurt for crop-level. Final model reduces median CER 6.64\%→1.24\% (printed) and 74.87\%→20.55\% (handwriting) and beats Typhoon OCR v1 7B on all five eval sets.}

\section*{Multilingual Speech Processing}

{\small\textbf{Alignment-Free Text-Audiobox for Voice Dubbing and Full-Duplex Dialogue Synthesis}} {\scriptsize[\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03992}{arxiv}]}\\
{\scriptsize Sanyuan Chen, Min-Jae Hwang, Sho Inoue, Anna Sun, Bokai Yu, David Kant, Dongmin Hyun, Dorian Desblancs, Gregory Antonovsky, Oleg Repin, Peng-Jen Chen, Xutai Ma, Zehai Tu, Juan Pino, Wei-Ning Hsu}\\

{\small\textit{A diffusion TTS model can learn text--audio timing without forced alignment, enabling better cross-lingual dubbing and natural full-duplex (overlapping) dialogue.}}\\[1pt]
{\footnotesize Eliminates explicit alignment/duration modeling in Audiobox-style generation by learning alignment via large-scale cross-attention, yielding one system for dubbing (incl. cross-lingual) and interactive dialogue with interruptions/backchannels. Latent diffusion (DiT + flow-matching) over DAC-VAE audio latents (\textasciitilde{}25Hz from 48kHz), text-conditioned via cross-attention; multi-diffusion for >1-minute audio plus multi-stage candidate reranking for perceptual quality. Alignment-free conditioning improves dubbing (prosody match, voice similarity, naturalness) over strong internal baselines and generates human-like overlap/turn-taking in short and long dialogues; emotion conditioning improves emotion match without harming realism.}

{\small\textbf{Confucius4-TTS: Transcript-Free Cross-Lingual Zero-Shot TTS with a Learnable Speaker Encoder}} {\scriptsize[\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11650}{arxiv}]}\\
{\scriptsize Huaxuan Wang, Huimin Wang, Ruiyu Zhang, Yingjie Li, Yitao Duan}\\

{\small\textit{Cross-lingual zero-shot voice cloning can be transcript-free by learning a speaker encoder from reference audio.}}\\[1pt]
{\footnotesize Confucius4-TTS enables transcript-free speaker prompting for cross-lingual TTS by extracting timbre from self-supervised speech features, making in-the-wild cloning practical while staying competitive with transcript-based systems. Two-stage TTS: text→semantic token predictor, then semantic→acoustic generator via conditional flow matching to mel + vocoder; a learnable speaker encoder maps SSL features from reference audio to a timbre embedding, trained end-to-end. Transcript-free prompting preserves intelligibility/quality: 3.73\% average WER on CV3-Eval (six directions) and best average overall rank in internal cross-lingual human eval versus open-source/commercial baselines (naturalness + speaker similarity).}

\section*{Foundation Model Theory}

{\small\textbf{The Head Complexity of Boolean Functions in Single-Layer Attention}} {\scriptsize[\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04046}{arxiv}]}\\
{\scriptsize Rajmohan Rajaraman, Ravi Sundaram, Amanuel Tesfaye}\\

{\small\textit{In single-layer self-attention, head count is a true compute bottleneck---extra width/precision can't replace missing heads.}}\\[1pt]
{\footnotesize Defines head complexity and proves a strict parity hierarchy: k heads compute k-bit parity but cannot compute (k+1)-bit parity even with unbounded dimension/precision; adds lower-bound tools and a compactness theorem (bounded dim/precision suffice when computable). Algebraic analysis of one-layer multi-head attention: clear softmax denominators to get rational/polynomial forms; an alternating-sum cancellation shows k heads force monomials omitting some variable, giving zero correlation with (k+1)-bit parity; discretization yields compactness. Unconditional separation: k heads suffice for k-bit parity and are insufficient for (k+1)-bit parity regardless of embedding dimension/precision. Also: any n-bit Boolean function is computable with 2\textasciicircum{}n heads, while almost all need \textasciitilde{}2\textasciicircum{}n/n\textasciicircum{}2 heads (near-tight worst-case).}

\section*{LLM Evaluation}

{\small\textbf{Clean Engineering, Unstable Measurement: A Preregistered Reliability Failure of Black-Box LLM Observers on Shared Endpoints}} {\scriptsize[\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04198}{arxiv}]}\\
{\scriptsize Haoyaun Zhu, Jie Zhang}\\

{\small\textit{Black-box LLM judges on shared APIs drift over time---even with identical prompts---making automated eval/leaderboards unreliable unless measured in situ.}}\\[1pt]
{\footnotesize Preregistered validation showing the core assumption behind LLM-as-judge on shared endpoints (repeatable scores/rankings for the same model name) fails; proposes identity levels plus design/reporting practices to detect and mitigate drift. Two preregistered campaigns: same-window repeats and next-day byte-identical replays; measure rank agreement (Spearman) and exact-match vs fixed thresholds (0.90/0.99). Follow-ups vary label wording, gap size, providers, and self-hosting to isolate causes. Across \textasciitilde{}53k requests, same-window rank agreement is \textasciitilde{}0.40 (vs 0.90 target) and next-day replay \textasciitilde{}0.78 (vs 0.99), despite correct logging/replay. Label wording can shift outputs as much as the signal; many comparisons fall below the judge's noise floor; common fixes don't restore reliability; self-hosting helps only with tightly controlled serving---so reliability must be measured in the exact deployment setting.}

\section*{Field Overview}
{\footnotesize Today's batch is dominated by foundation-model engineering for long-horizon/agentic use: at least \textasciitilde{}25--35 papers on post-training and agent scaffolding (RLVR/GRPO/OPD variants, process rewards, trajectory evaluation, tool-use harnesses, terminal/GUI agents, proactive agents, and long-memory systems like RuleMem/MemoryLACE/VoiceMem). A second major cluster is efficiency/serving and compression---roughly \textasciitilde{}20--30 papers on KV-cache eviction/compression, speculative decoding, quantization (FP4/2-bit), pruning/LoRA/merging, and long-context attention optimizations (e.g., VestigeKV, Random Attention, SGD-KV, GrowPage, FlashAttention FP4). The most striking ``surprising'' concentration is audio: well over 100 papers spanning large audio-language models, TTS/voice agents, speech enhancement, codecs/tokenizers, and deepfake detection/watermarking/forensics (multiple new benchmarks like AT-ADD, TAG-Bench, AudioSpan, VocalAffectBench, StreamAV-Bench), suggesting audio is having a benchmark-and-systems moment similar to last year's VLM surge. Safety/trustworthiness is also heavily represented (\textasciitilde{}20+), with multilingual jailbreak robustness, watermarking, privacy/PII leakage, unlearning, evaluation realism/LLM-judge reliability, and auditing (e.g., IndicSafeEval, Counter-GEO-Bench, Rent-a-RAG, LeakageBench, Clean Engineering/Unstable Measurement).}
